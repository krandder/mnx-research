Equilibrium, Bayesian Prices and Money Pumps

Three properties an automated market maker can have: it is the pricing rule of a strategic equilibrium; its fill prices are Bayesian estimates; no sequence of trades and waits can take money from it. This page states exactly how they relate, with the theorems, the counterexamples, the smallest game that has a decaying transient and is safe, and the impossibility results that fence the space.

22 September 2026 · three independent gpt-6-astra runs at maximum reasoning (J: martingale first, K: game first, M: the space), a literature map of 40 papers, and the adversarial reviews of the withdrawn rule · sources at the end

1. The three properties, made precise

The market maker has a state x, a price R(x) for the next unit, a trade map, and an autonomous evolution during waits. A pump is a finite schedule of trades and waits by one trader, ending flat, whose cash is positive; since the market maker's response is deterministic, so is the cash. Three strengths of "no pump" matter:

  • N₀: no pump from a flat, settled book.
  • N_cyc: no profitable loop that returns the whole state.
  • N∗: no pump from any state, in particular from a book that already holds inventory or a standing skew.

Two readings of "Bayesian": B_obs, each fill is priced at the conditional expectation of value given the public history and that fill, with every finite path having positive probability; and B_ctl, each fill is the conditional expectation under the trader's own strategy's law, with order ownership and the trader's private randomization in the conditioning. And E: the rule is the dealers' pricing in an equilibrium with an insider, noise, and a strategic uninformed trader who is a player.

2. The fundamental theorem

Theorem (J.3, K J.1, M J1; all three runs). N₀ holds if and only if there is a finite storage function G on the inventory-augmented reachable states with G(rest) = 0, G ≥ 0 on the flat fiber, every fill charged exactly ΔG, and G nonincreasing on every wait. The canonical choice is the minimum cost of reaching a state from rest. The equivalence needs reversible integrated fills and finite-cost liquidation from every reachable state.
Theorem (M J2, M M3, J Prop. J.4). N∗ holds if and only if the liquidation value of any inventory never rises during a wait. For a spreadless rule with reversible execution this forces the touch price not to move during a wait. So no spreadless market maker whose transient relaxes on its own is safe from every state: a trader who starts on a displaced book can always harvest the relaxation.

This settles a question that ran through the whole invariant program. The reversal-against-a-standing-skew leak (operator decision 11) is not a gap in a particular rule; it is the price of having a transient at all. Any rule with decay can be safe from rest and on full loops, never from every state, unless friction pays for it: a spread, or an execution surcharge that widens with the trader's own flow.

3. Does a Bayesian price protect the market maker?

Only under the controlled-law reading. If each fill is the conditional mean of one claim under the trader's actual law, with his own orders identified, then every strategy of his has expected cash ≤ 0, so no deterministic pump exists (J Thm J.1, K J.2, M J3). Full support is not needed for this.

The observational reading does not protect. A dealer who prices every fill at the exact public posterior, with every path possible, can still be pumped: in the six-slot example two slots carry information with probability ¾ and the others are fair coins; a trader who knows which slots are his own trades exploits the misattribution and earns a fixed positive amount (J Counterexample J.1, from F). This is the failure Goldstein–Guembel exhibit in print, where an uninformed trader profits from knowing his own past non-trades.

The converse fails both ways. A safe rule need not be any posterior: a price with a plateau admits no martingale at all (J Counterexample J.2), and a deterministic schedule executed at posterior fills costs exactly zero, so a rule that charges a strictly positive round-trip cost, as every safe rule with decay does, is not B_ctl (K Thm L.2). "Bayesian" and "unexploitable" are cousins, not the same property.

4. Does an equilibrium protect the market maker?

Not by itself. A Nash equilibrium gives the manipulator a lower bound of zero, not an upper bound. K's two-date game with an insider, an uninformed strategic trader and noise has correct posteriors and per-fill zero dealer profit, and the uninformed trader earns a strictly positive amount; J's six-slot ticket game does the same. What carries a proof is a specific list: fill-inclusive right-endpoint execution including the quadratic-variation term, posterior-mean pricing as the per-fill zero-profit condition, actual-law consistency for the uninformed trader, order-ownership information, and a separate deviation inequality on top of the equilibrium conditions (K Thm K.3). Drop any one and there is a counterexample.

5. The smallest game with a decaying transient that is safe from rest

A time-changed nonlinear Kyle–Back market (K Thm K.2–K.3; J Thm K.1 and M Thm M1 are the finite-horizon twins). One insider knows Z ~ N(0, v₀) and the value is h(Z); noise trades with variance v_t = v₀e^(−κt), so the market's uncertainty runs down on a clock; risk-neutral competitive dealers; a strategic uninformed trader who, in equilibrium, does nothing. The insider trades at rate κ(Z − Y); the dealers' price is H(v, Y) = E[h(Y + √v·ξ)], a function of two scalars: cumulative flow Y and the clock v.

cubic value map h(z) = a·z + b·z³: R = a·Y + b·Y³ + 3·b·v·Y permanent part a·Y + b·Y³ a function of cumulative flow, never decays transient part 3·b·v·Y decays with v, exactly exponential in calendar time safety N₀ with unbounded positions, via G = ∫H, when H_y(v,y) ≥ H_y(v,0) not N∗: a displaced start is harvestable, as Theorem M3 requires

Read against the operator's three features: the state is cumulative flow plus a clock, so a new trade does not replenish the transient; the transient decays because uncertainty is exhausted, not because an alarm fades; and the settled price depends on Y alone, so there is no timing-dependent permanence. It is a decaying transient with a permanent nonlinear part, derived rather than designed, and it is safe from rest. It is not the alarm model.

6. The space: steepening is possible in equilibrium

The claim "a finite-dimensional Bayesian equilibrium price is linear or saturating" is false. Take the value map g(y) = b·y − (b − a)·L·arctan(y/L) in the finite-horizon Kyle–Back game; the price H(t, y) = E[g(y + σ√(T − t)·ξ)] is odd, strictly convex for y > 0, has slope rising from a at the touch to b far out, a liquidity floor 1/b, and no saturation (M Thm M1, J Prop. M.1). It is safe from rest with no cap and fails from displaced states, as it must (M Thm M2).

What it lacks are the two replenishment features: the state is cumulative flow and a depletion clock, so a fresh trade does not restart the transient, and the settled response is a function of position only. Whether both can be had in an anonymous game is open, and the impossibility results say where not to look:

  • M3a: the original architecture, a fair that moves with displacement while the skew decays to zero, fails N₀ for every non-constant gain, cap or no cap (the ratchet).
  • M4: an all-state-safe one-factor Brownian Kyle price is affine.
  • M5: appending an exponentially weighted flow state to a smooth Kyle model with freely controlled rates forces the price not to depend on it at all.
  • M6: with three or more possible values, no scalar exponential-family belief closes under silence; an exposure coordinate is needed, which is F Theorem 4 seen from the belief side.

The standing conclusion. N∗ is not available to any rule with a self-relaxing transient and no friction, so the guarantee a market maker can honestly claim is: safe from rest and on full loops, with the spread as the friction that pays for displaced starts. The operational test that follows is not "random loops from rest" but "random loops from settled books with inventory", which is the test that caught the withdrawn rule. Today's rule passes it because its fair does not move between fills.

8. Open questions, ranked by the three runs

  1. Solve the anonymous Poisson-episode game with a persistent strategic uninformed trader: existence plus a deviation inequality, or a supported profitable deviation.
  2. A replenishing finite-state analogue of the steepening equilibrium (exponentially weighted flow, positive floor, timing-dependent permanence), or an impossibility wider than M3a and M5.
  3. Whether the withdrawn rule's integrated-fill contract can be implemented in a competitive game, and its exact recoverable-cash value.
  4. The smallest exact closed belief state for episodic activity, with cash-error bounds for compressing it.
  5. The least costly friction per safety standard: uniformly bounded spreads cannot buy N∗; a spread widening with own flow (Fruth–Schöneborn–Urusov) can.
  6. A finite-lot approximation of the continuous game that preserves the quadratic-variation cash term.
  7. An operational guarantee for a fresh trader entering a displaced book.
