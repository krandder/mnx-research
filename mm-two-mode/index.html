Two-mode quoting: reshape the ladder, don't split the maker

Small orders at tight spreads, blocks at wide spreads — the goal is right, but under MNX's uniform-price batch auction it is a ladder-shape question. Two separate MMs buy nothing the shaped ladder doesn't, and cost capital, ops, and inter-vault churn.

The mechanic that decides everything

MNX matches in ~200ms batch auctions at one uniform clearing price per batch, with pro-rata allocation (no price priority among eligible makers). Verified in the match engine, this changes how "size-dependent spreads" work compared to a continuous book:

  • A block pays the marginal price on ALL its units. A large market buy clears at the ladder level where cumulative depth covers its size — and every unit fills there, not at the walk-the-book average. The ladder's shape is the size→price schedule.
  • The tight front quote is never picked off cheaply. In the batch a block lands in, the MM's tight level fills at the same (wide, marginal) clearing price as the deep levels — strictly better for the maker than a continuous book.
  • Small orders genuinely get the tight price when they arrive alone: clearing lands on the front level (tie-break is closest-to-oracle).
  • All resting depth aggregates into one clearing price — regardless of who posted it. This is why two MM accounts cannot produce different execution prices for different taker sizes: the exchange already merges everyone's depth. Two-mode can only be expressed through the shape of total resting depth.

The honest limit — slicing: a sophisticated block can split itself across consecutive 200ms batches and consume the ladder level-by-level, paying the book-walk average instead of the uniform marginal price. Between MM ticks (~3s; fills currently do not wake the maker, only fair-price moves do) the discrimination degrades to continuous-book behavior. Two-mode therefore taxes naive block flow; defending against slicers is a separate knob (fill-triggered wake, faster flow-impact repricing) — Stephen question 1.

Exchange-mechanics fact worth knowing regardless: a small retail order sharing a batch with a block pays the block's clearing price. If retail protection is a goal, that's a property of uniform-price auctions themselves — no MM design changes it.

Options compared

OptionMechanicsVerdict
(a) Two-segment single ladder — small tight front level, large wide back levels New per-segment config in mm-core (per-level notional weights don't exist today; the sizing curve is linear with a flat per-level cap). Capital scaling divides by segment weights; new knobs enter the sizing-generation hash for clean cancel-replace rollout; hysteresis matching needs no change. Recommended. One process, one vault, one flow-skew signal seeing all fills; zero capital fragmentation; margin funding already prioritizes front levels.
(b) Two overlaid quote sets, one account Exchange-side fine (self-trade prevention only cancels crossing same-account orders; same-side coexistence OK; one-leverage-per-market satisfied at 1x). But the MM's hysteresis matcher cancels anything off its own desired ladder — two independent processes on one vault shred each other every tick. Collapses into (a): one planner must emit both sets anyway. Not a separate option.
(c) Two separate MMs (retail-tight vault + block-wide vault) Mechanically possible (two child vaults, two lease/control/telemetry stacks). Real benefits: independent inventory regimes, doubled per-account open-notional headroom, blast-radius isolation. Not worth it. Uniform pricing means the second account changes no taker's execution price. Costs: capital fragmentation at 1x (every resting order reserves full notional), double ops surface, and a coupling trap — block fills allocate pro-rata across both vaults, blowing the retail vault's small inventory cap into reduce-only, whose clamped closing orders can then cross the block vault: the two vaults end up trading with each other on the public tape. The genuine benefits (cap headroom, isolation) are cheaper to get directly (raise the market's cap; per-market mode switches exist).

Recommended design (option a, concrete)

Starting parameters (per market)

  • Front: 1 level at today's effective_spread_bps (the tight retail number, e.g. 20–40bps on liquid markets), notional ≈ $250–500.
  • Back: 3–4 levels starting 150–300bps out, notional ≈ 5–10× front each, spacing 2–3× current grid (via level stride).
  • Capital split: ≈ 15% front / 85% back of the market's book allocation; capital scaling divides by the sum of segment weights.
  • Constraints to respect: the back segment must sit inside oracle±MTB (quotes outside the band are excluded from matching entirely) and inside the margin-health buffer on the adverse side.

Rollout path

  1. Segment fields in the MM config + piecewise sizing curve + segment-aware auto-depth (the invariant and goldens extend with it).
  2. Stamp new knobs into the sizing-generation hash → enabling produces one clean cancel-replace.
  3. Control-plane columns + audited operator function (same pattern as capital scaling).
  4. Null config = exactly today's behavior (dark-ship idiom) → enable kas-devnet → testnet → one mainnet market.

The three questions for Stephen

  1. Slicing economics. Two-mode discriminates against naive blocks only; rational size slices across batches and gets the book-walk average. Is that the intended target (probably fine — naive blocks are the toxic flow), or do we also want cross-batch defenses: fill-triggered MM wake (fills don't wake the maker today) and/or a higher permanent-impact fraction (currently 0.2) so consumed depth reprices faster?
  2. MTB headroom. Wide-segment quotes outside oracle×(1±MTB) are dead weight — excluded from matching. On the stock perps (±5%) a 150–300bps back segment fits comfortably; is the intended block spread ever wider than the band on any market? If yes, that's an exchange market-config discussion, not an MM change.
  3. Capital & the 1x cap. At 1x every resting order reserves full notional: 4 markets × 2 sides × ~$10k back segment ≈ $80k permanently reserved. Does vault equity support the intended block depth — and should the per-account open-notional cap be raised in step (else the PR #66 clamp silently shrinks the block levels)?
