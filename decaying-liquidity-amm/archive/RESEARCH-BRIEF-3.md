# Research brief 3: equilibrium, Bayesian prices, and money pumps

You are a mathematician working in market microstructure and mathematical finance. The object is a state-dependent automated market maker (AMM): a state x, a price for the next infinitesimal unit R(x), a trade map x → x + dq, and an autonomous evolution of x between trades (waits). A "sure-profit loop" (money pump) is a finite schedule of trades and waits by one trader, starting from a given state and ending flat, whose cash is positive on every path (it is deterministic given the schedule, since the AMM's response is deterministic). "Non-pumpable" means no such loop exists.

Files in this directory: M4-SAFETY.md (loop identity, the storage-function characterization, Theorems 1 to 8), DECAYING-LIQUIDITY-AMM.md and D-dynamics-full.md (mechanism theorems), G-storage.md (the storage-function class and the concrete rule "Choice B"), E-model.md and F-attack.md (Bayesian models whose exact posteriors are nonetheless pumpable; F's counterexamples to the naive martingale bridge; F Theorem 4: no rule on the state (φ, P, F) can be a posterior), WORKFLOW-MODEL-SYNTHESIS.md (a four-model panel reaching the same conclusions), LITERATURE-MAP.md, DECAYING_LIQUIDITY_AMM.md (the engineering proposal). Read what you need; do not redo what they prove; cite them by theorem number.

## The question

The operator wants to understand the relationship between three properties of an AMM:
(E) it is the pricing rule of a strategic equilibrium in a game with informed traders, uninformed (noise and possibly strategic) traders, and market makers;
(B) its transaction prices are Bayesian: the price of each fill is the conditional expectation of value given the market maker's information including that fill (prices form a martingale in the market maker's filtration);
(N) it is non-pumpable, as defined above, ideally with unbounded positions.

Known so far: (B) with full support (every finite trade path has positive probability under the market maker's model) and fill-inclusive quotes implies that every strategy measurable in public information has expected profit ≤ 0, hence (N); but F-attack.md shows that exact posteriors of natural models are pumpable when the strategic trader's path is outside the modelled flow or his fills are misattributed ("policy consistency" fails). (N) does not imply (B): G-storage.md's Choice B is non-pumpable by a storage function and is not a posterior of any asset-value model. (E) with competitive (zero-expected-profit) market makers and a strategic uninformed trader as a player would seem to force (N), since a profitable manipulation is a deviation the equilibrium must price. Make all of this precise.

## Deliverables

J. **Martingale first.** For deterministic-response AMMs with state, give the exact fundamental theorem: (N) if and only if what? Candidates: existence of a storage function (potential) as in M4-SAFETY.md; existence of a probability model in which fill prices are a martingale in the fill-inclusive filtration with full support; a no-free-lunch condition in the sense of Çetin–Jarrow–Protter (2004) supply curves and their extensions with resilience (Roch–Soner 2013, Bank–Kramkov 2015), Huberman–Stanzl (2004) and Gatheral (2010). State which implications hold, which fail, with proofs or explicit counterexamples (use the files' examples). Then the martingale reading of Choice B: is there any filtration and measure, on an enlarged state, under which its fill price is a conditional expectation of something (not necessarily a fundamental value)? If the storage function G is a value function, of what?

K. **Game first.** Write the smallest game whose equilibrium pricing rule is (a) state-dependent with a decaying transient and a permanent component, and (b) non-pumpable with unbounded positions. Ingredients to consider: informed traders with short-lived signals who compete (Holden–Subrahmanyam 1992), noise traders (Poisson per side), competitive market makers quoting the next lot inclusive of its own information (Glosten–Milgrom 1985, Back–Baruch 2004), AND a strategic uninformed trader (a manipulator) whose strategy is part of the equilibrium and whose paths the market makers' beliefs assign positive probability (Chakraborty–Yilmaz 2004; Jarrow 1992; Allen–Gale 1992; Kyle–Viswanathan 2008). Theorem to aim for: in any such equilibrium the manipulator's expected profit is ≤ 0, hence no sure-profit loop; identify exactly which assumptions carry the proof (fill-inclusive quotes, zero-profit market makers, full support, policy consistency) and show by counterexample that dropping each one breaks it. Then: what does the equilibrium pricing rule look like on the exact belief state, and what minimal state is needed (E and F say more than (φ, P, F)). Also address Kyle–Obizhaeva–Wang (2018) "smooth trading with overconfidence and market power", the one equilibrium model known to produce transient impact with exponential decay, and whether its equilibrium is pumpable.

L. **The modification.** Given a game whose equilibrium AMM is pumpable (E-model.md's regime model, or the four panel models), what is the minimal modification that makes the equilibrium AMM non-pumpable: adding the manipulator as a player; requiring policy-consistent beliefs; changing the quote convention from pre-trade to fill-inclusive; adding a spread; changing the decay target as in Choice B (the relaxation toward αP); bounding positions? For each, state whether the modified equilibrium exists, what the pricing rule becomes, and whether it is non-pumpable with unbounded positions. Try to prove: every non-pumpable AMM on a finite-dimensional state is the fill-inclusive posterior-mean price of *some* game with a policy-consistent manipulator, or exhibit one that is not (Choice B is the natural test).

M. **The space.** Characterize, as far as you can, the set of AMMs that satisfy (E), (B) and (N) at once, on finite-dimensional states, and in particular whether any of them has the operator's three features: a decaying net-flow state, a price response that steepens with displacement to a positive floor with no saturation, and a permanent update larger for trades arriving while displaced. Known members: the linear-Gaussian Kalman/Kyle case (linear, no steepening); binary Glosten–Milgrom (saturating). Give the best candidate with steepening, or prove none exists on a finite state.
