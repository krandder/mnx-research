# Research brief 2: the information model behind a decaying-liquidity AMM

You are a mathematician and market-microstructure theorist. Build the simplest explicit model of information arrival, adverse selection and noise trading in which a competitive (zero-profit) or Bayesian market maker's price is exactly, or to leading order, the AMM rule below, so that the rule's curve, decay and fair-price updates are outputs of the model rather than knobs. Write theorems with proofs, or explicit counterexamples. Plain prose. One markdown file. Numerical checks are welcome (bun or python are available); keep code minimal.

## The AMM rule to rationalize

State: φ (recent net directional flow, decays exponentially with time constant τ between trades), P (cumulative net flow, never decays), F (the fair, a state). Price for the next infinitesimal unit: R = F + T(φ), with T odd, increasing, and *steepening away from zero* (a liquidity density that is highest at the touch and thins with distance to a positive floor; explicitly no saturation, since a flat skew is an infinite wall of liquidity). A trade of dq moves φ and P by dq and pays the integral of R along the walk (retrace on the way back). The fair moves only at fills:

dF = ( S′(P) + λ·|T(φ)| ) · dq,   with λ·C ≤ 1 for a hard inventory cap C on |P|.

S is the slow curve (any increasing shape; S′ ≥ 0 is what a patient trade pays permanently); λ·|T(φ)| is the extra permanent update for trades arriving while the price is already displaced ("urgent" flow), in the trade's direction, whichever side.

Operator's story to be made precise: informed traders with a real, time-sensitive signal trade quickly (competition among informed traders forces urgency); inventory and noise trades arrive slowly; a burst of one-directional flow raises the Bayesian probability that someone knows something, which shifts the expected value (the temporary term T(φ)); if flow stops, the alarm decays to zero; trades that arrive while the alarm is high are more likely informed and therefore move the permanent estimate more; slow traders still carry some information (the slow curve, S′ > 0). |φ| acts as a volatility / adverse-selection estimate: the book is less liquid when |φ| is large, in both directions.

1. Loop identity and "no sure-profit loop from rest": with S charged at the live position P, any (T, S) is safe; the urgent term is safe iff λ·C ≤ 1 with a hard cap and unsafe without one; a fair that keeps a share ρ of the skew released by decay is unsafe for every curved T unless ρ·T′ is constant; the handoff S(P − φ) needs S′ ≤ inf T(x)/x.
2. The martingale bridge (state and prove it cleanly): if the transaction price of every fill is E[v | history including that fill] under a probability model in which every finite trade path has positive probability, then every uninformed trading strategy has expected profit ≤ 0, hence no deterministic-profit loop exists from any state. Consequently a pricing rule that admits a sure-profit loop is not the posterior of any such model. Discuss exactly what "including that fill" requires (Glosten–Milgrom prices vs Kyle's pre-trade price) and what happens with a spread.

## Clarification from the operator (binding)

The idealized model has NO inventory cap. The cap in the loop theorem is only the market maker's operational maximum position and must not be used by the model to obtain safety. Consequently the fill-time update law λ·|T(φ)|·dq is at most an approximation valid inside a cap; the exact posterior update must be pump-free with unbounded positions, and the operator expects it to have a different form (possibly quadratic in the displacement, or whatever Bayes' rule gives). Derive that exact law; report the λ|T| rule only as the small-displacement or capped approximation, with its error.
