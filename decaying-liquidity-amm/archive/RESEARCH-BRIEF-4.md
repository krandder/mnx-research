## The operator's closure condition

A pricing rule is acceptable only if, in the game where a strategic uninformed trader U is a player with full knowledge of his own orders, U's best response is to do nothing (U ≡ 0), and the dealers' pricing is the conditional-mean price under the resulting equilibrium law. K.2–K.3 achieve this in a Gaussian game with a clock. The operator wants the same closure added to the non-Gaussian models that have the alarm feature: the posterior variance rises on a burst of one-way flow and falls in silence, and the price response steepens with displacement to a positive floor.

## Deliverables

N. **U added to the episode models.** Take (a) E-model.md's regime model (hidden activity A ∈ {0,1} with birth/death rates, noise ε per side, slow informed ν, active informed μ, value jumps with a Laplace or three-point prior), and (b) the tilted-regime model of WORKFLOW-MODEL-SYNTHESIS.md (Poisson episodes that exponentially tilt Poisson noise). Add U: no fundamental information, knows his own orders and private randomization, chooses any finite schedule of lots and waits, objective E[V·U_T − Σ fills] with U_T = 0 for round trips. Dealers quote each lot fill-inclusive at the exact posterior on the model's belief state. Questions: (1) Is U ≡ 0 a best response, or does U have a profitable deviation (the six-slot and own-history pumps of F and Goldstein–Guembel are the templates)? Give the deviation and its profit, or the proof of the deviation inequality. (2) If U profits, what change to the dealers' rule restores U ≡ 0: policy-consistent beliefs that include U's deviation set; a spread; a spread widening with own flow (Fruth–Schöneborn–Urusov); lot-level right-endpoint execution with a quadratic-variation-like term; conservative updating (the posterior update after a lot capped by what the lot paid)? For each candidate, whether the equilibrium exists, what the price becomes, and whether U ≡ 0. (3) In the repaired game, does the price keep the alarm feature (variance responding to flow, decay in silence) and the steepening?

O. **The lot-level deviation inequality.** K.3 proves U ≡ 0 by an Itô/storage argument for continuous finite-variation strategies against a smooth price H(v, Y). State and prove the discrete-lot analogue: for a dealer rule that prices lot k at p_k = E[V | H_{k−1}, lot k] under the equilibrium law, and a U who knows which lots are his, the condition on the pricing rule under which every finite U schedule has E[profit] ≤ 0. Identify why the six-slot example violates it (time-varying informativeness plus own-lot knowledge) and what rule property excludes it (candidates: the posterior update from a lot never exceeds the adverse-selection charge on that lot; "self-financing updates"). Relate to M4-SAFETY's storage function: is the discrete deviation inequality the same as the existence of a storage function on the belief state?

P. **Adversarial.** For every equilibrium constructed in N, attack it: own-history pumps, timing pumps around episode death, pumps using the decay in silence, two-account pumps, and pumps from displaced starts. Report the strongest with its profit; if none, a proof sketch.

Q. **Synthesis for the operator.** One page: the smallest non-Gaussian game with U as a player and U ≡ 0 in equilibrium whose price has the alarm and the steepening; its belief state (how many numbers); its pricing rule in closed form or as an explicit filter; its safety status (from rest, full loops, from displaced starts); what it costs (spread, friction) and what remains open. If no such game exists in the class considered, say exactly which assumption blocks it and what the nearest existing game is.
