# Synthesis: the information model behind the decaying-liquidity AMM

Four candidate models were built and each was checked by three independent verifiers (Bayes, features, loop safety). This note picks one, says what it gives the operator, says what it cannot give, and says which claims are proved, which are derived under stated approximations, and which are conjecture. Every number quoted below was re-run on this machine (`/tmp/pump-sim/synth_check.py`, `/tmp/pump-sim/synth_laplace_loop.py`, plus the verifiers' scripts) unless marked "reported".

The one-paragraph answer. Adopt the tilted-regime model (candidate "regime", model G): informed episodes that start and end at Poisson rates and exponentially tilt Poisson noise flow, priced at the posterior mean. It is the only candidate whose state is exactly a function of net flow at trades (buy then sell is the identity), it has closed forms for every AMM knob, and its fill-time permanent update is an explicit function the operator can use: quadratic in the displacement near zero, linear in |T| in the growth regime, and steeper than that near certainty. But the binding clarification (an exact posterior update that is pump-free with unbounded positions) cannot be met by this model or by any of the other three, and the reason is structural, not a defect of one candidate: a fair that moves more per unit for urgent flow than for patient flow, together with a displacement that decays in silence, is a convex permanent impact that a patient reversal does not undo, and that is a money pump (Huberman and Stanzl 2004, and Theorem 4 / equation (18) of the previous round). Cap-free safety requires the fair's long-run value to be a function of the position alone. So the operator's feature (iii) can live in the transient curve, or inside a hard cap, or in a relaxation-target rule, but not in a Bayesian fair used without a cap. Sections 1 and 4 make this precise.

## 1. The model to adopt

### 1.1 Primitives

Continuous time. The value is v_t = v_0 + Sigma_t + A_t J_t, where Sigma_t is the sum of the jumps of completed episodes, A_t in {0,1} says whether an informed episode is active, and J_t is its jump. Nothing is ever announced; the market maker learns only from its own fills and from silence.

Episodes. From the quiet state an episode starts at Poisson rate alpha, with J drawn from a symmetric prior pi. It ends at Poisson rate delta, independently of the flow; after it ends the jump is permanent (it enters Sigma) and the informed traders are gone. Two priors are used: the three-point prior J in {+j, -j} (everything in closed form) and a Laplace prior of scale b with informed intensity proportional to |J| (needed for the far field, section 1.3).

Noise. Uninformed buys and sells are independent Poisson streams at rate eps per side, unit size; the continuous-size limit is taken where stated.

Informed flow, the one non-standard primitive. During an episode with jump J the sign process is exponentially tilted: same-direction orders arrive at rate eps e^{theta_J}, opposite-direction orders at rate eps e^{-theta_J}, with theta_J = log(1 + kappa|J|/eps). Economically: competing informed traders add same-direction orders at rate kappa|J| (intensity proportional to the mispricing, the Holden-Subrahmanyam / Back-Baruch reduced form) and intercept a share kappa|J|/(eps + kappa|J|) of the opposite-side noise, so the pool sees the opposite side at rate eps^2/(eps + kappa|J|). For the three-point prior write mu = kappa j and theta = log(1 + mu/eps). This tilt is what makes a buy and a sell exact inverses on the posterior (section 1.2); the standard additive model (informed orders add to the same side, the opposite side stays at eps) does not have that property and is churn-manipulable (section 2).

Market maker. Competitive, risk neutral, no inventory objective, no cap. Every fill is priced at E[v | history including that fill]. With unit orders this is the Glosten-Milgrom quote; in the continuous-size limit the marginal unit is priced at the current posterior mean and a fill pays the integral of the posterior mean along the walk, which is the AMM's convention.

Example parameters used for every number below: j = 1, alpha = 0.02, delta = 0.2, eps = 1, mu = 2. Derived: theta = 1.0986, s = 1.3333, r = 0.1304, tau = 0.652, o_inf = 0.00652 (exact rest odds 0.00660), A = 0.00567, lambda_c = 0.1648, S' = 0.00187, T'(0) = 0.01246, n_sat = 4.58.

### 1.2 State and the decaying net-flow statistic

Exact state (three-point prior). Five numbers: the odds o_+ = P(active, +j | H)/P(quiet | H), o_- likewise, and the three per-regime conditional means E[Sigma | quiet], E[Sigma | +], E[Sigma | -]. (The candidate said "o_+, o_-, Sigma_hat"; the Bayes verifier showed Sigma_hat alone is not enough off pure-trade paths, because a fill reweights regimes carrying different completed-jump means. On pure-trade paths from quiet the three means coincide and the pair (o_+, o_-) is the whole state.)

Update at a fill. A buy of size dq multiplies o_+ by e^{theta dq} and o_- by e^{-theta dq}; a sell does the reverse. Derivation: the likelihood ratio of a same-direction unit is (eps e^{theta})/eps = e^{theta}, of an opposite-direction unit (eps e^{-theta})/eps = e^{-theta}, and the normalizer cancels in odds. Write u_± = log(o_±/o_inf). Then at trades u_+ increases by theta dq and u_- decreases by theta dq, so along any pure-trade path from quiet u_+ = -u_- = theta n with n the net signed flow. Buy then sell is the identity on the state. Checked: a walk of buy 3 then sell 3 costs 7e-16 (zero); the state after "+++-" equals the state after "++". So the AMM's phi = u_+/theta is exactly the net signed count at trades. This is derived from the tilt, and the tilt is assumed; the condition it needs is lambda_buy(A) lambda_sell(A) = lambda_buy(N) lambda_sell(N) for every hypothesis, which is a knife-edge on the interception share. The calibration test in section 5 is what falsifies it.

Update in silence. Between orders,

  d log o_J/dt = -(delta + s_J) + alpha pi(J)/o_J + alpha - delta sum_J' o_J',

with s_J = eps(e^{theta_J} + e^{-theta_J} - 2) = kappa^2 J^2/(eps + kappa|J|); three-point: s = mu^2/(eps + mu). Derivation of s: an active episode raises the total order rate from 2 eps to eps(e^theta + e^{-theta}) = 2 eps + s, so a silent interval of length t carries likelihood ratio e^{-s t} for "active" against "quiet"; independently the episode may have ended (rate delta). So the log-odds fall at rate delta + s. At small alarm the equation reduces to du/dt = -(delta + s)(1 - e^{-u}): for |u| below about 1 this is exponential decay at rate delta + s, i.e. the EWMA with tau = 1/(delta + s); for u >> 1 it is linear decay in nats, a floored CUSUM, and a burst of n units is forgotten in time about n theta/(delta + s), not tau log n. So the single-phi EWMA is exact at trades and exact in silence only near quiet. With a continuum of J each size has its own kill rate: tau_J = 1/(delta + kappa^2 J^2/(eps + kappa|J|)), so larger displacements decay faster and a single tau is exact only for the three-point prior.

### 1.3 Price formula and the shape of T

Exact price (three-point):

  p = v_0 + Sigma_hat + j (o_+ - o_-)/(1 + o_+ + o_-).

Fair. Define F as the silence limit of the price (the price the pool would converge to if no order ever arrived again). By construction F changes only at fills and T := p - F decays to zero in silence; this "silent-limit lemma" holds for every Bayesian fill-only model and is the model's version of the operator's bookkeeping. The filter computes F exactly; the closed form at small alarm (alpha -> 0) is

  F = v_0 + Sigma_hat + j r (o_+ - o_-)/(1 + r(o_+ + o_-)),   r = delta/(delta + s).

Derivation of r: in silence the unnormalized weight of the active state decays at rate delta + s; the delta share transfers to the quiet state carrying ±j into Sigma_hat, the s share is killed by the missing flow; integrating over the silence leaves the r-weighted odds. The closed form is within 1-2% of the exact silence limit (0.02267 against 0.02293 after three buys); the gap is the new-episode rate alpha during the silence and unequal per-regime means, both O(alpha/(delta + s)). All numbers below use the exact silence limit.

Pure-trade path from quiet with net flow n: o_± = o_inf e^{±theta n}, so

  p - F_0 = 2 j o_inf sinh(theta n)/(1 + 2 o_inf cosh(theta n)),

exact at every alarm level. Prices after 1..7 fast buys: 0.0172, 0.0553, 0.1509, 0.3482, 0.6158, 0.8278, 0.9352. At small alarm T(phi) = 2A sinh(theta phi) with A = j(1 - r) o_inf = j alpha s/(2(delta + s)^2): flat near zero (T'(0) = 2A theta = 0.0125, proportional to the episode frequency alpha, i.e. Bayesian shrinkage: a lone unit is almost surely noise), then exponential steepening as each same-direction unit multiplies the odds of an episode by e^theta = 1 + mu/eps.

Far field. The three-point prior saturates at ±j (increments 0.017, 0.038, 0.096, 0.197, 0.268, 0.212, 0.107, ... -> 0): an infinite wall of liquidity, the shape the operator rejects. Saturation is forced by any bounded prior, because E[J | flow] cannot exceed the support. The positive far floor needs two things together: an unbounded prior with an exponential tail, and an informed intensity that grows with |J| (a constant intensity reveals sign and presence but never size, so T saturates at E|J| for every prior). With a Laplace(b) prior and intensity kappa|J|, the posterior after an instantaneous burst of n from rest is proportional to pi(J)(1 + kappa J/eps)^n, whose mode is J* = n b - eps/kappa: linear in n with slope b. Computed on a wide grid (b = 1, kappa = 2, alpha = 0.02, delta = 0.2, eps = 1; 600 grid points to |J| = 80): price increments per unit 0.016, 0.059, 0.315, 1.316, 2.082, 1.437, 1.098, 1.034, 1.020, 1.015, ... -> 1.00 = b. So: steepening, then a linear far field with density floor 1/b, never saturating. Gaussian prior for contrast: increments 0.014, 0.035, 0.119, 0.361, 0.675, 0.610, 0.362, 0.238, ... falling like 1/sqrt(n) (concave far field); bounded uniform prior: 0.022, 0.084, 0.352, 0.870, 0.778, 0.307, 0.114, ... -> 0 (saturating). This is the tail dictionary; the filter candidate's Tweedie identity T'(z) = Var(J | z)/s^2 makes it a theorem for Gaussian-scale-mixture priors (posterior variance of the jump rises with the surprise iff the tail is heavier than Gaussian).

One departure from the operator's curve that must be reported: the density is not monotone. Between the touch and the far floor it dips below the floor (minimum density 0.48/b at n = 5 in the run above, i.e. an increment of 2.08 per unit against the far 1.00) and then recovers. The reason is generic: the presence branch is exponential and the size branch is linear; the exponential must overshoot the line before the mass moves to the size branch. For steady flow at rate R instead of an instantaneous burst the far field is different: the posterior mode is J ~ (R - eps)/kappa (reported: R = 4 -> 3.13, R = 8 -> 6.85), i.e. slope 1/(kappa tau) per unit of flow. A one-state AMM can match one of these two far slopes, not both (open question 4).

### 1.4 The exact fill-time update law, and how it differs from lambda |T(phi)| dq

Exact law. dF is the change of the silence limit under the fill map o_± -> o_± e^{±theta dq} (together with the per-regime means). It has no simpler exact closed form; the filter evaluates it.

Small-alarm, continuous-size law, general state. Differentiate F = j r (o_+ - o_-) (small odds) along the fill map: dF = j r theta (o_+ + o_-) dq. Writing T_± = ±j(1 - r) o_± for the two same-side alarm displacements (T = T_+ + T_-),

  dF = lambda_c (T_+ + |T_-|) dq,   lambda_c = r theta/(1 - r) = delta theta/s = delta (eps + mu) log(1 + mu/eps)/mu^2.

Checked off pure-trade paths against the exact silence limit with dq = 0.05: 2 buys then wait 1: 0.00342 exact vs 0.00336 law; 3 buys, wait 0.5, 1 sell: 0.00571 vs 0.00566; 3 sells, wait 0.3: 0.01594 vs 0.01678. So at small alarm the law is within 1-5% and it is a function of two states, not one. The sign is the trade's direction whichever side the alarm is on: a buy against a sell-alarm raises F by lambda_c |T_-| dq because it lowers the sell-alarm (0.0159 per unit after three sells).

Pure-trade path from quiet (u_+ = -u_- = u). Then T_+ + |T_-| = 2A cosh u and T = 2A sinh u, and cosh = sqrt(1 + sinh^2) gives

  dF = sqrt(S'^2 + lambda_c^2 T^2) dq,   S' = 2 A lambda_c = j alpha delta theta/(delta + s)^2.

The floor and the alarm term are the cosh and the sinh of the same log-odds. This is the "something else" the operator allowed. Compared with the rule dF = (S' + lambda |T|) dq:

- Near zero it is quadratic in T, dF/dq = S' + lambda_c^2 T^2/(2 S') + ..., not kinked. So "maybe it's squared" is right near zero, and the square is of T, with coefficient lambda_c^2/(2S'). The kinked rule over-charges by 33% after one buy and 10% after two.
- For |T| >> 2A = 0.011 (a one-sided alarm) the two coincide: linear in |T| with slope lambda_c.
- After a silence the floor grows to S' e^w, w = (u_+ + u_-)/2 >= 0, because both alarms carry evidence; the pure-path form under-predicts there (0.00258 against 0.00342 after 2 buys and a wait of 1) while the two-state form does not.
- Beyond n ~ 3 the exact law leaves both forms behind, upward. Along the pure buy path, dF/dq measured at the state after 0..7 buys: 0.0019, 0.0032, 0.0088, 0.0253, 0.0692, 0.161, 0.262, 0.246, against the hyperbolic law 0.0019, 0.0031, 0.0081, 0.0212, 0.0466, 0.0727, 0.0725, 0.0461 (errors -2, -5, -8, -16, -33, -55, -72, -81%). The local permanent share dF/dp is 0.13, 0.14, 0.15, 0.18, 0.28, 0.62, 1.71, 3.78: once the alarm is near certain (n above about 5.5 = n_sat + 1) the fair moves more than the price per unit, because every past fill is re-attributed as informed. So the exact law is not of the form lambda |T| for any bounded lambda; lambda_c is its small-alarm slope only, and the permanent share r_eff = r(1 + o_+ + o_-)/(1 + r(o_+ + o_-)) rises from r = 0.13 at rest to 1 at certainty.

Unit orders. With unit fills the per-unit law is side-asymmetric: a buy at rest moves F by j r o_inf (e^theta - e^{-theta}) = 0.00227 (exact 0.00231), because the same-side odds move by e^theta - 1 = 2 and the opposite-side odds by 1 - e^{-theta} = 2/3. Neither delta/eps + delta/mu = 0.30 nor lambda_c = 0.165 reproduces this; keep lambda_c for the continuous-size limit only.

Cross-model agreement. The other three candidates, with different primitives, put the same shape on the update: the Gaussian-bucket presence model fits its Monte Carlo update as a|T| + bT^2 with b/a ~ 0.1 (same-side exponent 1.4); the filter model's one-shot spike-and-slab gives an excess gain p(1 - p) z^2/s^2, quadratic in the implied displacement inside the dead zone, a hump at the transition, then full attribution; the episodic Kyle model gives S' + (mu/eps) T on continuation trades. "Quadratic near zero, linear in |T| through the growth regime, steeper than linear at certainty" is therefore a robust output, not a feature of one parametrization.

### 1.5 Decay mechanism

Silence lowers the alarm for two independent reasons, both multiplicative on the odds: the flow an active episode would have produced did not come (rate s), and the episode may have ended (rate delta). The temporary component decays to zero: after three buys T/T_0 = 0.711, 0.500, 0.242, 0.054 at t = 0.25, 0.5, 1, 2, against e^{-(delta + s)t} = 0.682, 0.465, 0.216, 0.047 (slightly slower, by the logistic factor 1 - P(active)); at high alarm the rate is delta + s P(quiet), so a saturated alarm is sticky. F is exactly constant in silence (0.02293 at every t above), by its definition as the silence limit. It decays to F, not to the pre-burst price: the share r = delta/(delta + s) = 0.13 of a small burst's displacement is read as "informed, now done" and stays. Limits: delta = 0 gives r = 0, everything temporary, F never moves (the M0 rule); s = 0 (informed orders replace noise one for one, so silence carries no information) gives r = 1, T = 0, pure permanent impact as in Kyle or Glosten-Milgrom. A decaying T therefore requires informed activity to raise the total order rate, and the size of the decaying share is s against delta. Silence is evidence of noise only for the s/(delta + s) share.

### 1.6 The floor for patient flow

S' = j alpha delta theta/(delta + s)^2 = 0.00187 per unit: episode frequency, times permanent share, times evidence per unit, times jump size, divided by the square of the alarm relaxation rate. Derivation: at quiet both alarms sit at odds o_inf = alpha/(2(delta + s)); a single patient unit is informed with those odds, multiplies the same-side odds by e^theta, shifts the expected value by about j theta o_inf, and the r share of that shift is permanent. It is state-independent (at quiet), so S(P) = S' P is linear and is charged at the live position: Kyle's lambda, Guéant's potential, safe by Theorem 5. It is the cosh of the quiet log-odds, i.e. the same episodic population at rest odds, not a separate slow class; an always-present slow informed class with intensity nu and jump scale j_s would add a second linear Kyle term of the standard form (asserted, not derived here). A curved S'(P) does not come out of the model; it would need the prior on |J| to depend on the pool's own inventory.

## 2. Why the alternatives lose

All four models share the decisive fact (section 4): their exact posterior-mean price admits a sure-profit loop on the quiet path with unbounded, or moderately bounded, positions. So none meets the binding clarification, and the choice among them is on everything else. The adopted model wins on four counts: its state is exactly net flow at trades (so it has no wash pump with zero waiting time, which two of the others have); every knob has a closed form checked to 4-5 digits by two verifiers who rebuilt the filter independently; its update law is explicit and its error against the operator's rule is tabulated; and its safety numbers were reproduced (first pump at C ~ 3.7 under walk pricing) rather than conjectured.

Episodic sparse-jump Kyle model (candidate "kyle-heavy"; Bayes 0.55 flawed, features 0.72, safety 0.40). Its Poisson special case is the additive model: an informed buyer adds buys and never sells, so a sell carries no information about a buy-episode and the state is (Psi_+, Psi_-) with nothing ever divided. Consequences found by the verifiers: (a) a burst of n buys followed immediately by n sells is a sure profit for n >= 2 + 2 eps/alpha at Glosten-Milgrom post-trade quotes (+0.017, +0.224, +1.57 for n = 3, 4, 6) and for every n >= 1 under the AMM's walk convention, with no waiting at all: an Allen-Gale trade-based manipulation that the tilted model excludes by construction (its churn costs exactly zero); (b) with the unbounded size prior that steepening requires, the per-loop profit is unbounded (reported +5.2, +46, +114, +162 for n = 4, 8, 16, 32); (c) the growth-regime curve T(phi) = (a kappa alpha/2 rho^2)((1 + alpha/eps)^phi - 1) is not the posterior outside the linear regime (two histories with the same additive phi give T = 0.0169 and 0.0132); (d) the update law is one-sided (reversals pay only the floor), which is the pump; (e) the "binary GM reference" in its script is the two-sided ladder and prints a gain where the text claims a loss. What to keep from it: the silent-limit lemma; the presence scale C* = log(2 rho/kappa)/log(1 + alpha/eps); the argmax tail rule for burst responses; Back's quantile map G^{-1}(Phi(y/s)) as the Brownian dictionary (bounded saturates, Gaussian linear, Laplace quadratic, Gaussian-tailed slab gives a dead zone then a linear floor); and the sharp criterion it exposes: a fill-only posterior is wash-pumpable with no wait iff its state does not retrace under buy-then-sell.

Three-regime presence model with Gaussian flow buckets (candidate "uncertain-informed"; Bayes 0.72, features 0.72, safety 0.40). Its direction log-odds are odd in the bucket flow, so a churn pair (+y, -y) leaves the direction unchanged; but the attribution of past fills does not retrace: with persistence, a single contrary bucket is cheapest to explain as noise inside a live episode (P(+ -> 0 -> +) = d a/2 = 0.0005), so e_+(3) = 1.66 on the buy and e_+(-3) = -0.04 on the sell, and F ratchets up on zero-net-flow churn. The safety verifier's loop at the candidate's own example parameters (build 20 x 3, 20 churn pairs, 40 silent buckets, unwind) nets +210 with position 60, growing without bound in the build size; the smallest pumping position found is 36, and the reflected loop restores F. Its Powell search over 5-10 bucket paths could not see this. Further defects: the "exact" odds recursion omits the denominator (1 - a) + d(o_+ + o_-), which is what actually kills a saturated alarm (from pi_+ = 0.998 one silent bucket leaves odds of 6.3, i.e. pi_+ = 0.86, where the stated recursion predicts odds in the hundreds), so the "constant log-odds drift, sticky near 1" mechanism is wrong; the touch-slope formula misplaces the (1 - beta lambda_w) factor and uses the prior alarm instead of the posterior at zero flow; the martingale bridge is proved with incompatible hypotheses (the trader's position H-measurable and his flow buried in the Gaussian noise); the density has a 3-9x thin band at the presence flip that survives when the anticipation lump is negligible; and the model is discrete-time by necessity (in the Brownian limit the regime is read off the quadratic variation). What to keep: the separation of the direction channel (odd in flow) from the activity channel (squared flow feeds the alarm level, not its direction), which says that gross activity must not enter the AMM's direction state; the Monte Carlo fit of the update law (a|T| + bT^2, b/a ~ 0.1) as independent support for section 1.4; and the criterion that reversals must be decisive.

Two-clock filtering model, OU transient plus jump permanent (candidate "filter"; Bayes 0.60, features 0.62, safety 6/10). Its linear-Gaussian case is exact and valuable: R = mhat_0 + lambda_m P + g phi with lambda_m = sigma_m/sigma_u and g from a closed-form Riccati root (verified to six digits), (phi, P) exactly sufficient, tau = 1/kappa. That case is the safe class of the loop theorem, and it has neither steepening nor a displacement-dependent permanent gain, which is the cleanest statement of the trade-off in section 4. Its heavy-tailed case, the only one with features (ii) and (iii), fails on four points: (a) the claimed decay "d thetahat = -kappa thetahat dt exactly, for any prior" evaluates the Ito filter on the measure-zero silent path and drops the Wong-Zakai correction; the robust filter on a silent path adds the drift -Cov_t(x, (h - hhat)^2)/(2 sigma_u^2) to every posterior mean, which vanishes only for a Gaussian posterior, so the fair drifts in silence (-12% of the post-burst fair over one tau on the candidate's own grid) and "F moves only at fills" fails; (b) in the variant where urgency means transient information (v1), the within-burst permanent gain falls to zero and goes negative (explaining away through Cov(m, theta) < 0), the opposite of feature (iii); (c) the one-shot map T(z) is the posterior of a static surrogate, and the parameter map's far floor 1/(beta_theta Delta_b) is inconsistent with its own numbers because informed flow proportional to the residual mispricing compounds; (d) it is heavily pumpable: a single symmetric round trip of 8 units over 10 tau with a wait nets +3.39, 10 units +24.7, 6 units over 2 tau +16.9, and a held position of 4 with fast-in/slow-out cycles is enough in v2. What to keep: the FKK gain formula (the marginal price per unit is the posterior variance of the live jump), Tweedie's T' = Var(J | z)/s^2 with the scale-mixture monotone-likelihood-ratio proof of the tail dictionary, and the leak identity Pi = integral P (Gamma_t - Gbar) dP - integral Theta dP, which is the general form of the pump argument in section 4.

The adopted model's own defects, all now stated in section 1: the closed-form F is the alpha -> 0 limit (1-2% off); Sigma_hat moves at fills off pure paths (five-number state); the unit-order lambda check in the candidate's text was mislabeled; the pump search used post-fill unit pricing and depth 8, so it missed the cap-4 sweep that walk pricing admits (3.7, not 6); the three-point prior saturates and the far floor needs the Laplace extension; the Laplace density has a dip; and the informed intensity kappa|J| and the tilt are primitives, not equilibrium outcomes (Back-Baruch would endogenize the intensity; Chakraborty-Yilmaz warn that an informed trader can also game a presence alarm).

A remark on "|phi| as a volatility estimate". In the tilted model gross activity carries no information about presence (only the net count does), and in the additive model gross activity is the manipulation channel. The only Bayesian effect of a higher estimated noise rate eps is a smaller theta, i.e. more liquidity in busy times. So the operator's intuition that the book should be thinner when gross activity is high has no support in any of the four models and, in two of them, is exactly the pump.

## 4. Loop safety and what replaces the cap

### 4.1 The martingale bridge, corrected

What is true (proved). If every fill is priced at p_i = E_P[v | H_i], then for weights q_i that are H-adapted and whose flow is part of what P models, E_P[sum_i q_i (v - p_i)] = 0 by the tower property. So the pool breaks even in expectation against its modeled population, and a modeled uninformed trader's expected loss equals the expected gain of the informed.

What is false (counterexamples in all four models). The brief's step "hence no deterministic-profit loop exists from any state" does not follow. A strategic trader who chooses his fills as a function of history is not the model's noise: under his own law Q each planned fill has likelihood ratio 1 across the hidden states, while the pool's price attributes it the modeled ratio e^{±theta}. His expected profit under Q is sum_k E_Q[dq_k (E_Q[v | H_k] - p_k)], the accumulated misattribution, and on the quiet path (no other flow, a path of positive probability) his cash is a fixed number. E_P[profit] = 0 is a sum over all flow paths; it is balanced by paths on which a real episode hits his position, money the pool never receives. Positive path probability is the wrong hypothesis. The right one is that the pricing map lie in the loop theorem's safe class (section 4.3). Witness in the adopted model: sell 4; wait 0.3; buy 8; wait 0.5; sell 8; wait 2; then four buys of 1 with waits of 15: cash +0.3076 j, maximum |P| = 4, F shifted by -0.269; wait and run the sign-reflected loop: same cash, F restored to 0 to six digits, total +0.615 j. Caps of 3 and 3.5 lose (-0.58, -0.30). Single burst / wait 8 / patient unwind: -0.16, -0.05, +0.06, +0.66, +3.24 for bursts of 4, 5, 5.25, 6, 8. With Glosten-Milgrom unit pricing (a spread paid on every unit) the first single-burst pump moves from 5.1 to 6 = 1/lambda_c; the spread raises the threshold and does not remove it. The AMM's walk convention is the less safe of the two.

### 4.2 The mechanism: convex permanent impact does not retrace

Leak identity (proved; it is Theorem 1 of M4-SAFETY in the form of its equation (3)). For any rule R = F + T with F the silence limit, the profit of a loop from rest that ends flat is

  Pi = integral P dF - integral T dP,

by integrating integral F dP by parts with P = 0 at both ends. The second term is the transient dissipation; it was a cost in every run here and is provably nonnegative when T is a retracing potential dissipated by decay (Theorem 2), which is the single-phi case. If dF = gamma dP with a constant gain gamma, the first term is gamma times a closed loop integral of P dP, which is zero: the linear-Gaussian filter, and Kyle. Otherwise the first term is the position-weighted permanent movement, and it is positive whenever the gain is larger while the position is being built than while it is being unwound.

The burst / wait / unwind inequality (proved as stated). Let F_b(x) be the fair after a fast burst of x from rest, D(n) the transient paid on the burst, and gamma_f the per-unit fall of the fair during a patient unwind after a full wait (the after-silence floor, S' e^w). The burst costs integral_0^n [F_b(x) + T_b(x)] dx; the unwind, sold at the current fair, returns at least n F_b(n) - gamma_f n^2/2. Hence

  Pi >= integral_0^n [F_b(n) - F_b(x)] dx - D(n) - gamma_f n^2/2.

For a linear fair with the same slope on both legs, F_b(x) = gamma x and gamma_f = gamma, the first and third terms cancel and Pi <= 0: linear permanent impact is safe. For a posterior-mean fair with a sparse prior, F_b is flat near zero and rises steeply past the presence scale, exactly the operator's features (ii) and (iii): a lone unit moves the fair by the floor S', a burst moves it by much more per unit. Then the bracket exceeds the floor cost and grows with n. With a bounded jump prior, F_b(n) -> j and the single-trip profit is bounded (about 3-4 j here: +3.24 at n = 8); unboundedness comes from repeating burst cycles against a held position (the mechanism of Theorem 4; reported under walk pricing: a 6-unit burst nets +4.25 j against a held position of 10 and +7.84 j against 20). With an unbounded prior, the prior that feature (ii)'s far floor requires, F_b(x) ~ b(x - n_sat) beyond the dead zone, the bracket is ~ b(n^2 - n_sat^2)/2, and a single round trip is unbounded: computed for Laplace b = 1, kappa = 2 (fast burst, wait 20, one unit sold per 8): -0.05, -0.73, +7.96, +27.6, +64.6, +116.9 for n = 2, 4, 6, 8, 12, 16, leaving F at +14.3 after the 16-unit trip (the reflected loop restores it and doubles the profit). This is Huberman and Stanzl's theorem seen from inside the model: a permanent impact that is nonlinear in the trade, with the two legs separated in time, is a quasi-arbitrage.

The structural statement. Put the two facts together:

- Feature (iii), "trades arriving while displaced move the fair more", says the fair's response to a burst is convex in the burst (per-unit permanent impact rising with the alarm).
- Feature (i), decay of the displacement in silence, is what lets the unwind be priced at the floor instead of retracing the burst.
- Therefore (ii)+(iii) with (i) is the pump, for every posterior-mean rule, unless the reversal undoes the burst's permanent movement at the same per-unit rate, which means the fair's long-run value is a function of the position P alone.

Proved parts: necessity of a constant permanent gain in the single-phi design class (D-dynamics equation (18): k + h = lambda, and M4-SAFETY Theorem 4: every lambda > 0 unsafe uncapped); sufficiency of "fair is a potential of P" (Theorem 5, M3) and of the relaxation-target rule (Theorem 8); the inequality above for any rule with a convex burst response and a floor-priced unwind. Exhibited numerically: quiet-path pumps for all four candidate posteriors. Conjectured: no fill-only Bayesian model with a sparse prior and Poisson or Gaussian noise has a pathwise loop-safe posterior mean with unbounded positions (open question 1). The linear-Gaussian filter is the only exactly safe posterior among everything examined, and it has neither (ii) nor (iii).

### 4.3 What replaces the cap

The Bayesian pool has no cap and does not need one against its modeled population, because a pumped fair is mispriced and the modeled informed traders trade against it (a probabilistic defence). An AMM facing an adversary cannot invoke that. Two scales in the adopted model play the cap's role, and neither is sufficient alone:

- 1/lambda_c = s/(delta theta) = mu^2/(delta (eps + mu) log(1 + mu/eps)) = 6.07 units: the position at which the small-alarm law lambda |T| leaks (Theorem 4's lambda C <= 1), about mu/delta for mu <= eps, one episode's worth of informed volume.
- n_sat = log(1/o_inf)/theta = 4.6 units: the burst at which the alarm saturates and the permanent share goes to 1. A sweep across the reachable width 2C (Theorem 4's |phi| <= 2Q) reaches certainty once 2C > n_sat, and the held position C monetizes the shift. This is what the cap-4 loop does (2C = 8 > 4.6), and it is why the operational threshold (3.7 under walk pricing) is below 1/lambda_c.

So the rule "lambda C <= 1 with lambda = delta theta/s" is necessary and not sufficient for the exact posterior. Three designs are provably safe; the operator picks by what feature (iii) is allowed to mean.

(A) Cap and clip. Keep the Bayesian shape inside a hard cap and clip the fill update: dF = min(exact, lambda |T|) dq with a fixed lambda and lambda C <= 1, and C also below the sweep threshold (search on the calibrated filter; 3.7 here, conservative rule 2C <= n_sat gives 2.3). Theorem 4 proves safety inside the cap; the clipped rule departs from the posterior only in the certainty region. This keeps feature (iii) in the fair and pays for it with the cap.

(B) Fair as a potential of position (M3). F = F_0 + S(P) with S charged at the live P, any S including a convex one, plus T(phi) with the full steepening burst response (T_AMM(n) = p_b(n) - F_0 - S' n, the chord curve of section 1.3, or its monotone envelope). Safe for every T and S with unbounded positions (Theorem 5), no cap. What changes: "urgent flow moves the fair more" becomes "flow at a large position moves the fair more, and unwinding gives it back"; a burst's displacement beyond S' P decays entirely. This is the only cap-free option that keeps the curve, and it is the safe projection of the posterior: the pricing error it makes is exactly the retained share r of a burst, 13% of the small-alarm displacement here.

(C) Relaxation-target rule (Theorem 8, rule (37) or (40)). S = T-type handoff where the decay pulls the retained state toward a fixed fraction of the position (phi -> 2P/3 for the cubic curve). Safe uncapped, with a nonzero handoff after a burst; but in the long run the retained part is again a function of P, so a patient reversal undoes it. It gives permanence against silence, not against reversal.

In every safe design the long-run fair is a function of P. That is the theorem, and it is the whole answer to "what plays the cap's role": nothing in the posterior does; the safe designs remove the need.

### 4.4 Wash pumps and the retrace criterion

Separately from the wait-pump, a fill-only posterior admits a zero-wait wash pump (buy n, sell n at once) iff its state does not retrace under buy-then-sell. The adopted model retraces exactly (tilt), so churn costs zero under the walk convention and loses under unit pricing (reported -0.2 to -4.7). The additive model does not retrace and is wash-pumpable at every cap >= 3 (candidate "kyle-heavy"); the Gaussian-bucket model retraces in direction but not in attribution, and is churn-pumpable through the ratchet (candidate "uncertain-informed"). Any AMM state that reads gross volume (VPIN-like alarms) fails this criterion. The operator's rule passes it by construction; that is the one safety property the posterior-mean framing does not have to buy.

Three-point prior, small alarm, continuous size; continuum-prior versions in brackets. Calibration from observable flow.

1. eps: the per-side order rate in quiet periods.
2. mu (or kappa and the size scale): inside identified bursts, the same-side rate is eps + mu and the opposite-side rate is eps^2/(eps + mu). The second number is the test of the tilt: the additive model predicts the opposite side stays at eps. If the opposite side does not fall during bursts, the retrace property is not in the data and the AMM's net-flow state is a design choice rather than a posterior.
3. delta: 1/(mean burst duration). alpha: burst frequency per unit time.
4. j (or b): the mean permanent post-burst displacement divided by r = delta/(delta + s). The tail of the size distribution of permanent moves decides the far field (exponential tail: linear floor 1/b; Gaussian: concave; bounded: saturating).
5. Consistency checks that over-identify (delta, s): the post-burst decay rate of the quote displacement should be delta + s, and the retained fraction should be delta/(delta + s). Two observables, two unknowns; a mismatch says the three-point / single-tau approximation is failing (continuum priors decay faster for larger displacements).
6. C: not a calibration; a loop search on the calibrated filter with aggregated orders up to 2C, walk pricing, and the two-sided sweep family (scripts /tmp/pump-sim/lens_loop_G.py, lens_search.py, synth_check.py), reported for both pricing conventions.

## 6. Open questions, ranked

1. Is there any fill-only Bayesian model with a sparse prior (steepening), decay in silence, and a pathwise loop-safe posterior mean with unbounded positions? Conjecture: no; the burst/wait/unwind inequality forbids it whenever a patient reversal is priced at the floor. The two escape routes named by the verifiers are both unsolved: (a) episodes that end when the price has adjusted (informed intensity falling in the remaining mispricing), which would make a reversal after adjustment decisive; (b) a two-sided insider whose sells discredit the buy alarm at the reciprocal rate. Route (b) is the tilt and does not escape (section 4.1). Route (a) is caveat 5 of the adopted model and is the most valuable unsolved computation. Also open: the Brownian episodic model (Gaussian noise, presence odds by integrating over episode start times) retraces instantaneous round trips, but nothing shows it survives the wait-pump.

2. Sharp capped threshold for the adopted model under the AMM's walk convention. The search found 3.7 (sweep) and 5.1-5.25 (single burst), with depth-limited families; a lower one may exist. Needed: exhaustive search with aggregated orders up to 2C, waits on a grid, sequences beyond 24 actions, both conventions; and an analytic bound replacing "2C <= n_sat" (which is conservative by a factor 1.6 here).

3. Endogenize the informed intensity and the tilt. kappa|J| and the interception share are primitives. A Back-Baruch-type equilibrium with presence uncertainty and Poisson death would say whether the reciprocity condition (net-flow sufficiency) is an equilibrium outcome or a knife-edge, and whether the informed trader manipulates the presence alarm (Chakraborty-Yilmaz). Calibration step 2 is the empirical test in the meantime.

4. The continuum far field for a one-state AMM. The burst slope b and the steady-flow slope 1/(kappa tau) differ; a single phi matches one. Matching both needs a second state (burst rate). And the density dip (0.48/b at n_sat): adopt the exact curve or its monotone envelope T_env = min(2A sinh(theta phi), phi/b - const)? Both are safe under M0/M3; the envelope under-responds by at most the overshoot. The operator's "slightly different curve" allowance probably covers the envelope.

5. Quantify the pricing error of the three safe designs against the posterior on typical flow (not only quiet paths): (A) cap-and-clip, (B) M3 with the chord curve, (C) the relaxation-target rule. The M3 error is the retained share r per burst (13% here); the others are not computed.

6. Silence drift of the fair off pure paths. F is exactly constant in silence in the adopted model, but Sigma_hat moves at fills after silences (five-number state). The size of the resulting departure from a single-phi rule on typical mixed paths is not quantified.

7. Spread and tick. Unit pricing raised the single-burst threshold from 5.1 to 6. A minimum spread or tick in the AMM is a cheap safety margin whose size against the pump family is not computed.

8. Identifiability of delta versus s from post-burst decay and retained share when episodes overlap or the prior is continuous; and the gamma-prior-on-eps extension (busy times), which in the tilted model only lowers theta.

Ledger. Proved: net-flow sufficiency at trades given the tilt; the silence ODE and the rate delta + s; the exact pure-path price formula; the saturation theorem for bounded priors and the tail dictionary (via Tweedie); the small-alarm laws for T, S', lambda_c and the hyperbolic pure-path form; F constant in silence; the corrected bridge; the leak identity; the burst/wait/unwind inequality; safety of designs (A), (B), (C) by Theorems 4, 5, 8; necessity of a constant permanent gain in the single-phi class. Derived under approximation: the closed-form F (alpha -> 0, 1-2%); the EWMA (small alarm); the error table for the update law; the Laplace far field on a finite grid. Numerical witnesses, not theorems: the pumps (cap-4 sweep, single bursts, Laplace round trips) and the threshold 3.7. Conjecture: open question 1.
