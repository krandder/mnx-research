A Decaying-Liquidity AMM Without Money Pumps

Concentrated liquidity at the touch, a transient that fades when flow stops, a fair that learns more from urgent flow than from patient flow, and a proof that no sequence of trades and waits from a flat book can take money from the market maker, with no inventory cap. The rule is three numbers of state and a handful of closed-form functions.

Withdrawn, 22 September 2026, the same day. Two adversarial reviews of this page ran after publication. The theorem holds exactly as stated, and that is the problem: it covers schedules that start from a flat book (P = 0) and loops that return the whole state. From any settled book where the market maker holds inventory, a trader who starts flat can run a repeatable pump: sell in small slices where the fair's gain h is large, wait, buy back where it is small. Reproduced independently: from (P, φ) = (20, 10) with the parameters used in the figures, 211.7 bp·units per cycle, five identical cycles, the fair ratcheting down 12.5 each time; from P = 0 the same schedule loses 22.5; today's production rule costs the trader 490 on the same fills. Profit scales with the inventory squared, so no parameter or spread fixes it, and a market maker is almost never flat. The design is kept below as the record of what was tried and why it fails.

1. What we asked for, and what turned out to be impossible

The market maker quotes one curve. Its state is the standing flow φ (recent net buying in units), which decays between trades; the position P; and the fair F. The price of the next unit is R = F + T(φ), with T steepening away from zero, so liquidity is dense at the touch and thins with distance to a positive floor. Trades walk the curve and retrace it. That part is settled and safe on its own (Alfonsi–Schied 2010; our M0).

The open question was the fair. We wanted it to move at fills, more when the price is already displaced (urgent flow carries more information), to be pump-free against every loop a single trader can run from rest, and to need no inventory cap. Three runs proved independently that this combination does not exist as stated:

Theorem (G Thm 4, E Thm 4, F Thm 1). If φ decays toward zero by any law (exponential, nonlinear speed, volume clock) and the fair is frozen during waits, then every non-constant update dF = h(φ)·dq that grows with displacement, whether linear in |T|, quadratic, or anything else, admits an exact profitable loop from rest with unbounded position. The witness parks a resting position, cycles a small traversal while the fair ratchets, and unwinds; profit grows linearly with the number of cycles.

The reason is structural: a fair that responds convexly to a burst and is unwound patiently after the transient has decayed is nonlinear permanent impact in the sense of Huberman–Stanzl (2004), and nonlinear permanent impact is always a pump. Two exits exist. Keep an operational cap, in which case dF = λ|T(φ)|dq with λ·C ≤ 1 is safe (sharp). Or do not decay the transient all the way to zero.

2. The rule (withdrawn; see the notice above)

The second exit gives an exact rule (G, "Choice B"). The transient relaxes toward half the position rather than toward zero; with that one change there is a unique fill-time update compatible with the curve, and a storage function that proves safety with no cap.

Every fill is charged the exact increment of a storage function G = P·F − s·P²/2 + V(φ) + B(φ) − P·A(φ), and every wait lowers G at rate (2h(φ)/τ)·(φ − P/2)². Hence:

Theorem (G Thm 5, eq. 37). For every finite schedule of fills and waits from rest that ends flat, the trader's cost is V(φ_end) + B(φ_end) + (2/τ)∫waits h(φ)(φ − P/2)² dt ≥ 0. Every full-state loop from any state costs its dissipation. No inventory bound is used. Fill-then-immediate-inverse restores the state and returns exactly the opposite cash.
Why that is not enough. G = P·F − sP²/2 + V(φ) + B(φ) − P·A(φ) is linear in F with coefficient P. It bounds the trader's cost only on the fiber P = 0; on every other fiber it is unbounded below, and P never decays. The reviewers' schedule from a settled book: eighty slices of 0.4 sold with waits of 6τ between them, each sold where h is large and each moving F down by (s + h)·0.4; then buy 6; then buy the rest back while pinning φ near zero, where h is small. The fair ends lower, the position ends where it began, and the trader keeps the difference. The astra review that tested the theorem's own hypotheses did not find it; the Claude panel did, and the reproduction script is listed in the sources.

3. What it does to a wave of flow

Read against the operator's story: the burst is charged the steep part of the curve and raises the alarm; while the trader holds, the alarm does not vanish but relaxes to a level set by the position; patient trades are cheap and mostly temporary; trades landing while displaced are attributed to information and move the fair. The one departure from the story is that with a position held the transient never decays fully: the settled price is F + T(P/2), and the fair F alone is not the settled price.

4. Where the shape comes from, and what is exact

The curve. A price response that is flat near zero and steepens with displacement is what Bayes gives when the value's innovations have a sparse or heavy-tailed prior: small imbalances are attributed to noise and shrunk, large ones are followed (E Thm 2–3: a Laplace jump prior with diffusive flow noise gives an odd, globally convex T whose slope tends to a positive floor). A bounded jump gives the saturating Glosten–Milgrom response instead, which is an infinite wall of liquidity at one price and is rejected. Back (1992), Glosten (1994), Back–Crotty–Li (2018) and Banerjee–Green (2015) are the published cousins.

The decay. That the alarm falls when flow stops is Easley–O'Hara (1992): an active informed trader would keep trading while mispriced, so silence is evidence the burst was noise. The exact sufficient statistic in every Bayesian model is richer than one number (a presence probability plus per-regime means); an exponentially weighted net flow is exact only in the Gaussian case, where T is linear. The rule above is therefore mechanical, not a posterior: its storage function is not the log-likelihood of any asset-value model (G §Bayesian reading; F Thm 4 shows no rule on (φ, P, F) alone can be a posterior, since a balanced buy-then-sell raises the alarm and the next price).

The fair update. The exact Bayesian fill law is quadratic in the displacement near zero, linear in |T| through the growth range, and super-linear near certainty (the panel's tilted-regime model: dF/dp runs from 0.13 to 3.8 along seven buys). No bounded λ describes it globally, and every exact posterior we built is pumpable with unbounded positions. In the rule above, h(φ) is the unique gain compatible with T under the shifted decay; λ|T(φ)| is its small-displacement approximation with λ_eff = d/(3aL) = 1.500 here and an O(φ²) error. Replacing the shifted decay by decay to zero while keeping any such λ reinstates the pump.

What is proved, what is not. Proved: the impossibility theorem; the storage certificate and the cost identity for the rule; the sharp λ·C ≤ 1 bound for the capped alternative; steepening from a Laplace prior. Derived under approximation: the decay time τ ≈ 1/(episode death + missing-flow rate) and the retained share of a burst in the regime model. Not established: a Bayesian equilibrium whose posterior is the rule; the rule's relaxation target α = 1/2 is a compatibility choice, not a primitive.

C had all three properties on paper and fails from settled books. What stands after the reviews: A is safe from rest with the cap load-bearing and its settled-book case is unchecked; B is today's structure and is safe from every settled book, because the fair is fixed between fills and the transient decays to zero. Whether any rule with a displacement-sensitive fair is safe from every settled book without a cap is now the open question, and it is what the equilibrium research is aimed at.

6. What would have changed in the market maker (not to be built)

  • One curve family T with four numbers (a, b, L, s) and τ, replacing the permanent/transient depth pair, the far-liquidity fraction, the band, and the saturation Q*. Q* is retired outright: a flat skew is infinite depth at one price.
  • State per market becomes (P, φ, F). The flow accumulator keeps φ and P; F is a stored state updated only at fills; the wait rule relaxes φ toward P/2 instead of toward zero.
  • The ladder inverts T from φ as today; the reservation is F + T(φ). Fills are charged the closed-form cost above, so the ladder sizes are the curve's increments as before.
  • The position enters nowhere except the relaxation target and the operational cap. The cap is not load-bearing for safety.
  • The exchange-mark average, if kept, is external information layered on top and sits outside the loop guarantee; it should follow the market, not our own quotes.
  • Invariant: the cost identity (eq. 37) as a test over random schedules on every market family, plus the fill-inverse identity.

Calibration, from the regime model

Quiet-time flow gives the noise rate ε. Inside bursts the same side runs at ε + μ and the opposite side at ε²/(ε + μ) (the tilt test; an additive model predicts ε). Burst duration gives the episode death rate δ, frequency gives the arrival rate. Then τ ≈ 1/(δ + μ²/(ε + μ)), the retained share of a burst is δ/(δ + s), and the touch and far slopes come from marginal price response near zero and at large displacement against a markout proxy. The value scale is not identifiable from flow alone.

7. Open questions, ranked

  1. A strategic equilibrium with controlled orders in which this rule, or a rule with one more belief state, is the market maker's best response.
  2. The minimal extra state that makes the exact Bayesian posterior pump-free, and its storage certificate.
  3. A two-state version of the rule with different fill and wait conventions (a rank argument shows none exists with these conventions).
  4. An identifiable unbounded-jump model with global steepening under finite-rate Poisson flow (the current derivation needs diffusive noise).
  5. Cash-level approximation errors of the three designs over realistic turnover, and the spread and tick margin they add.
